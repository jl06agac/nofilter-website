Verified, not claimed

Proof, not
promises.

Anyone can claim their coffee protects forests. Almost no one can show you. Every plot we buy from is mapped against official conservation boundaries — government forestry data, held and checked by the NGOs on the ground. Not a promise to trust; a record you can check.

Layer 01 · Live

Polygons

Each coffee plot, its buffer, and the forest beyond, mapped as GPS boundaries — partner-signed and held as data. The line between farmed and untouched isn’t a claim; it’s a coordinate.

Layer 02 · Live

Satellite

Every polygon is enrolled in Global Forest Watch — independent weekly radar and optical passes that flag canopy change, run by a system with no stake in what we source.

Layer 03 · Rolling out

Drone

Once a year, each landscape is photographed from the air along the same path — proof, over time, that it’s still standing. Rolling out across our origins through 2026.

Layer 04 · Rolling out

Ground evidence

Quarterly GPS-tagged photographs by our NGO partners — PRCF, SRI, YOSL-OIC and Fauna & Flora — confirming what the data shows from above.

About

Coffee with
a boundary to hold.

NoFilter started with a simple refusal: coffee shouldn't cost a forest. We buy single origins from the communities inside Sumatra's most pressured landscapes, roast them for the workplace, and route a fixed share of every sale to the people holding the line on the ground.

No offsets bought elsewhere, no vague pledges. The coffee is good enough to stand on its own — the conservation is the point, made visible.
