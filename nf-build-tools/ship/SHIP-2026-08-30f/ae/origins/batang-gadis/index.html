02OPTION AThe long read · editorial, signed

Why NoFilter exists · the whole argument

The difference between a forest lost and a forest held often comes down to a few dollars and cents.

NoFilter exists to direct more of what you pay directly to the people keeping it standing. The problem is, what you pay is already going somewhere — and not in the right direction.

Most of us are already funding the system that clears the trees. Because most of the coffee we drink is grown where it can be produced cheapest — and growing coffee cheaply usually means making the land as efficient as possible. Trees cleared. Shade removed. Coffee planted to the horizon.

It works.Farms produce more. Supply is predictable. Coffee stays cheap.

And because all of that happens long before the coffee reaches us, there's rarely anything on the bag — or in the cup — to tell us what our coffee is helping pay for.

NoFilter backs coffee from forests still standing.

We buy from the edges of forests that are still under pressure — places with species found nowhere else, and where coffee is already part of how people live.

A share of every sale goes to the NGO holding that boundary. By contract, on every invoice — not a donation at the end of the year.

The point is to help farmers build a living around the forest, rather than further into it.

The world doesn't need more cheap coffee. It needs livelihoods on the edge of the forests still under pressure.

Every coffee we source grows in the agroforestry that hems a forest under pressure — Batang Toru, Leuser, the Arakan highlands — ripening under standing canopy rather than on the bare ground left where forest falls. That agroforestry is the line itself: a living, working edge, tended by the people who farm it, that holds the clearing back. And it only holds while the coffee finds a buyer.

So your order is never a gesture — it funds the NGO partners who keep that edge alive, shelters the forest and the flagship species that call it home, and gives the families under the canopy a reason to let the trees stand.

Alex ClarkFounder, NoFilter · Batang Gadis, North Sumatra

The record

Sumatra’s Batang Toru forest is the last refuge of the Tapanuli orangutan. Safeguarding their habitat means investing in the farmers who border it.

Through wildlife conservation and agroforestry programmes led by local partners PRCF and OIC, NoFilter buys directly from smallholders in buffer villages like Sampean. To maximise community returns and coffee quality, processing is centralised in Tanjung Dolok through co-op wet-milling and drying infrastructure we’ve built on the ground. The result: exceptional coffee that turns sustainable agriculture into a frontline defence for wildlife.

The record

In the forested mountains of western Myanmar, under a living canopy shared with the western hoolock gibbon.

High in the forested Arakan Mountains of western Myanmar, coffee grows under a living canopy shared with the endangered western hoolock gibbon. These canopy-dwelling primates rely on dense, undisturbed forest cover to survive. To protect this fragile ecosystem, NoFilter partners with Fauna & Flora and the Asho Chin Coffee Association (ACCA). Together, we support shade-grown harvests that are certified Wildlife Friendly and PGS Organic — turning sustainable coffee production into a direct line of defence for critical watershed forests while strengthening local Asho Chin livelihoods.

The record

The buffer zone of Batang Gadis National Park, where tigers, tapirs and hornbills still move through the forest.

At the edge of Sumatra’s Batang Gadis National Park, ancient rainforest gives way to traditional coffee farms. This crucial buffer zone remains a vital corridor for some of the island’s most iconic species — where Sumatran tigers, Malayan tapirs, and hornbills still move through the forest. To safeguard this boundary, NoFilter partners with the Sumatra Rainforest Institute (SRI) to source coffee cultivated by smallholders farming alongside the park’s edge. By returning a dedicated conservation share from every sale back into local programmes, each roast directly supports forest protection and sustainable livelihoods at the frontline.

The record

On the edge of the Leuser Ecosystem — the last place orangutans, tigers, elephants and rhinos still share one landscape.

Nestled at the heart of Sumatra’s Leuser Ecosystem, Gayo Lues sits on the boundary of the only place on Earth where wild orangutans, tigers, elephants, and rhinos still share a single landscape. Protecting this megafauna realm relies on giving forest-adjacent communities legal authority over their native land. Through a two-decade partnership with local NGO YOSL-OIC, three local communities have secured legal guardianship of 2,000 hectares of village forest (Hutan Desa). With the groundwork laid and our inaugural coffee shipment landing this September, NoFilter helps turn community forest stewardship into a sustainable, long-term conservation economy.
