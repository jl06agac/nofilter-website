## The problem

The problem is, they're already going somewhere. And not in the right direction.

Most of us are already funding the system that clears the trees. Because most of the
coffee we drink is grown where it can be produced cheapest — and growing coffee
cheaply usually means making the land as efficient as possible. Trees cleared. Shade
removed. Coffee planted to the horizon.

It works. Farms produce more. Supply is predictable. Coffee stays cheap.

And because all of that happens long before the coffee reaches us, there's rarely
anything on the bag — or in the cup — to tell us what our coffee is helping pay for.

---

## The answer

NoFilter backs coffee from forests still standing.

We buy from the edges of forests that are still under pressure — places with species
found nowhere else, and where coffee is already part of how people live.

A share of every sale goes to the NGO holding that boundary. By contract, on every
invoice — not a donation at the end of the year.

The point is to help farmers build a living around the forest, rather than further
into it.

## Notes for whoever builds the page

- **Two H2s, not two H1s.** The page gets one H1; these are section heads.
- **"Most of us are already funding the system that clears the trees"** is the
  strongest claim here and the most challengeable. Sun-grown conversion is well
  documented, but have the source to hand before this goes public — same discipline
  that retired the m² figure.
- **Not written, deliberately:** "we pay more per kilo than the market asks." It is
  not evidenced anywhere in the build. If NoFilter does pay above market at the
  farmgate, it is a strong line and belongs in the answer section — but it needs to
  be true first.
- **"It works. Farms produce more. Supply is predictable. Coffee stays cheap."**
  Keep verbatim. Conceding the other side's case is what stops the passage reading
  as a lecture, and it is the hardest thing in here to write.
