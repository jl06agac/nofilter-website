NoFilter · layout reference brief · 1 August 2026

The section is a poster. It needs to be an argument.

Where the unified build lost ground against the old index.html, which references are worth your time, and what specifically to take from each.

Prepared against build 2026-08-01a

First, the diagnosis

You did not lose a style.
You lost a second column.

The old treatment and the new one carry nearly identical words. The difference is structural, and it is worth naming precisely before we go looking at other people's sites.

index.html · what works

Three stops for the eye

Eyebrow, then a ranged-left display headline, then a serif lede at 62ch, then a 52ch qualifier. Alongside it, in its own column, a three-up stat row with a 2px rule over each figure.

The reader lands on a claim, drops into the reasoning, and finds the proof sitting to the right of it. Every line starts at the same x position, so the eye always knows where to return. The stats are not decoration, they are the evidence for the claim.

nofilter-unified · what broke

One stop, then a wall

Headline centred at 18ch across four lines, on slate, with the film full-bleed underneath and no stats at all.

Centring destroys the return point: every line begins somewhere different, so the eye has no rail to run down and long copy cannot live there. Full-bleed media ends the section rather than supporting it, because nothing sits beside it to be read against. The evidence column was deleted outright, so the claim now stands unsupported.

Reference set · type-led editorial

Sites earning their type

These are the ones I would actually spend an hour in. Each is here for one specific reason, stated. I have not padded this with award galleries.

By-Kin

by-kin.com

UK studio, swept Awwwards Site of the Day, the Developer Award, FWA and CSS Design Awards. The reference point for confident editorial typography carried by weighted smooth scroll, where transitions keep visual continuity instead of cutting.

Take this Scale contrast without centring. Enormous display type that still ranges left, so it reads as a publication rather than a title card. This is the single change that would fix your section.

Uncommon Studio

uncommonstudio.com.au

Australian studio. Described as a confident grid that breaks at exactly the right moments, with GSAP transitions behaving like camera moves rather than effects.

Take this The discipline of breaking a grid on purpose. Your page currently has no grid to break, so every full-bleed moment reads as default rather than as emphasis. Establish the column, then break it once per chapter.

Obys · Design Education Series

des.obys.agency/typography-principles

Obys are a concept-driven studio doing narrative-heavy work, and they publish their typographic reasoning rather than just the output. The alignment episode is the relevant one: their line is that alignment changes how text is perceived and read, and that the choice is strategic rather than aesthetic.

Take this The argument for why the centring is the fault, from people who do this for a living. Also their font-pairing method, which is built on contrast and proportion rather than obvious matches. Your Big Shoulders and Source Serif 4 pairing is already sound, so this is about setting rather than choosing.

Seasoned by Koto

seasoned.koto.studio

Current Awwwards typography honouree. Koto are a brand studio, so this is type systems applied to a brand rather than a portfolio flex.

Take this How a brand voice survives being scaled from a wordmark to a full page of running copy. Directly relevant to getting NOFIL+ER and the survey-line device to work at page scale rather than only on packaging.

Groteskly Yours

groteskly.xyz

A type foundry's own site, also an Awwwards typography winner. Foundry sites are useful because they have to demonstrate type at every size at once, from 200px display down to 11px mono captions.

Take this The small end. Your 11px mono kickers and stat labels are doing a lot of structural work across the whole site and have never really been designed, only inherited.

Mat Voyce

matvoyce.tv

Kinetic typography, letters that stretch, snap and recombine on scroll. GSAP Site of the Year nomination.

Take this, carefully You already have kinetic type in the HOLD THE LINE glitch hero and the manifesto morph. This is the ceiling for that device. It is also a warning: Voyce can do this because the motion is the content. On your page the motion has to serve an argument, so one kinetic moment per page, not per section.

Reference set · the split-narrative pattern

Type one side, media the other

This is the pattern you described wanting, and it has a well-tested form.

Obama Foundation

obama.org

Split-screen with a fixed left panel anchoring the narrative while the right side moves. Five-item top navigation despite an enormous range of work.

Take this The anchor-and-scroll mechanic, which is the sophisticated version of what you asked for. Rather than a static video sitting to the right, the type column scrolls while the media sticks, so one clip can carry three or four beats of copy. Good use of your five editorial clips, which are currently spent one per section.

WaterAid

wateraid.org/uk

Hero video plus emotional storytelling, and critically, statistics paired with the narrative that explains them rather than floated as a standalone band.

Take this Stats welded to the sentence they prove. Your 5–10%, 4 forests and 100% figures are your strongest assets and the unified build dropped them entirely. They belong next to the claim, not in a separate impact section further down.

Farm Africa

farmafrica.org

A scroll-driven zoom that literally focuses attention on the organisation's core aim, plus dual navigation serving both browsing and goal-directed users.

Take this Agricultural-boundary subject matter handled without disaster imagery. Also the closest sector peer to the actual thing NoFilter does, which most coffee-brand references are not.

The sector peer worth studying for the opposite reason

Age of Union is the conservation site everyone points at, and it makes exactly the mistake your unified build makes. Full-bleed video manifesto, centred uppercase headlines, body copy left-aligned underneath, and on the homepage no numbers presented at all. It looks expensive and it argues nothing.

That matters for your read on the situation. You said the entire site is behind. On visual production values, perhaps. On structure, the conservation sector's ceiling is not high, and the old index.html section already beat most of it by putting evidence beside the claim. The gap is not that you are behind the field. It is that the unified rebuild moved away from the thing that was working.

What not to take

Three traps in this reference set

  1. WebGL and Three.js. Half the award winners are 3D showcases. That is a portfolio idiom. It costs a fortune in build and load time and it will not sell a coffee subscription to a facilities manager at Accenture.
  2. Centred uppercase as a default. It is everywhere in this set because portfolio sites have almost no copy to place. You have a real argument to make, which needs a left rail.
  3. Motion as texture. Smooth scroll and transitions read as craft when they are rare and as latency when they are constant. You already carry Lenis; that is enough.

Recommendation

Restore the two-column, then earn the media

Concretely, for the "what most coffee does" section, and then as the pattern for every chapter after it:

  1. Range the headline left and drop the 18ch cap. The index.html setting was clamp(38px,6vw,84px) at line-height:.92 ranged left, and it held four times the copy without strain.
  2. Put the film in a column at roughly 40–45% of the width, not full bleed, and let the type column run past it. Sticky is the upgrade once the static version is right.
  3. Bring the stat row back and place it under the lede in the type column, not in a separate band. Three figures, rule above each, mono label beneath.
  4. Keep the cyan scrawl. On slate it is one of the few accents that stays distinct for you, and it is now carrying the payoff rather than echoing it.
